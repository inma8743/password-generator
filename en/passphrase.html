What is a passphrase?

A passphrase is a password made from several random words. It can be easier to remember than a random string while still being strong when it is long enough.

How are the words picked?

Your browser’s secure random number generator picks independently from 7,772 English words. A repeated word is normal. Picking, editing, or rearranging words yourself changes the randomness shown here.

Based on the list by Joseph Bonneau and EFF, excluding four hyphenated words so separators mark clear word boundaries. Source and license.

If you used the older, small-list generator for an important account, replace that password with a new random one.

When is it useful?

  • For a password-manager master password, prefer your trusted manager’s built-in generator where available. Use six or more randomly selected words and never reuse it.
  • Wi-Fi, device, and local encryption passwords that should be memorable.
  • Check the site’s length limit. Don’t truncate a result; use your password manager’s random-character generator if a passphrase won’t fit.
